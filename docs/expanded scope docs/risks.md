# Known Risks & Design Dangers

## Purpose of This Document

This document lists **known narrative and mechanical risks** that could undermine *Common Ground* if not actively avoided.

These are not hypothetical.
They are dangers already identified through design exploration.

If any new feature approaches one of these risks, it must be reviewed carefully or reworked.

---

### Mechanical Risks

### 1. System Bloat
**Risk:**  
Adding too many parallel systems that do not meaningfully interact.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
Complexity dilutes intention and increases player fatigue.

**Mitigation:**  
- Prefer expanding existing systems
- New mechanics must touch at least two existing systems

---

### 2. Optimization Over Care
**Risk:**  
Players min-maxing metrics instead of engaging emotionally.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
Turns restoration into extraction.

**Mitigation:**  
- Diminishing returns
- Soft caps
- Emergent consequences instead of raw bonuses

---

### 3. Invisible Failure States
**Risk:**  
Players unknowingly triggering long-term negative outcomes.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
Feels unfair and erodes trust.

**Mitigation:**  
- Foreshadow consequences
- Use NPC reactions and environmental cues

---

### 4. Hard Fail States
**Risk:**  
Game-over conditions that halt progress entirely.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
Contradicts the theme of persistence and repair.

**Mitigation:**  
- Failure reroutes, never deletes progress
- Systems degrade instead of collapse

---

## Tone & Presentation Risks

### 5. UI Breaking Immersion
**Risk:**  
Overly explicit meters, alerts, or numerical dominance.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
Turns lived experience into dashboards.

**Mitigation:**  
- Prefer qualitative feedback
- Use world changes before UI indicators

---

### 6. Leaning Too Far Into Horror or Comedy
**Risk:**  
Letting either dominate the experience.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
Both extremes break tonal balance.

**Mitigation:**  
- Horror stays implied
- Comedy stays gentle and human

---

### Narrative Risks

### 7. Over-Explaining the Cosmology
**Risk:**  
Explaining how gods, oaths, chips, or the Divine Engine work in explicit detail.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
Mystery is a core pillar of the game.  
Clarity collapses awe and turns metaphysics into rules-lawyering.

**Mitigation:**  
- Use partial accounts
- Conflicting testimonies
- Observed effects instead of explanations

---

### 8. Gods Becoming Characters Instead of Forces
**Risk:**  
Giving gods too much screen time, personality, or dialogue.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
Gods are systems, pressures, and consequences — not NPC quest-givers.

**Mitigation:**  
- Gods act through intermediaries
- Direct appearances are rare and costly
- Divine presence should feel wrong, heavy, and disruptive

---

### 9. Undermining Player Care
**Risk:**  
Mocking, trivializing, or punishing the player for gentle or compassionate choices.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
The game’s emotional contract is built on trust.
Breaking that trust invalidates the entire experience.

**Mitigation:**  
- Consequences escalate systems, not morality
- Failure complicates, it does not shame

---

### 10. Turning the Story Into Power Fantasy
**Risk:**  
Framing the player as “chosen,” exceptional, or destined.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
*Common Ground* is about **collective care**, not heroic supremacy.

**Mitigation:**  
- The village succeeds together
- Progress comes from maintenance, not domination

---

### 11. Romance Overpowering Theme
**Risk:**  
Allowing romantic subplots to eclipse the game’s central questions.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
Relationships are lenses, not destinations.

**Mitigation:**  
- Romance must reinforce themes of choice, restraint, or sacrifice
- No “perfect” endings without cost

---

## Production Risks

### 12. Lore Drift Over Time
**Risk:**  
Small changes accumulating into contradiction.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
Erodes internal consistency and player trust.

**Mitigation:**  
- Keep `lore.md` authoritative
- Update locked decisions immediately when changed

---

### 13. Solo Knowledge Silos
**Risk:**  
Critical understanding living only in one person’s head.

**Why It’s Dangerous:**  
Makes the project fragile.

**Mitigation:**  
- Archive decisions
- Write post-mortems for major changes

---

## Final Reminder

*Common Ground* is fragile by design.

It is easy to make it louder, faster, clearer, or more “gamey.”
It is hard to keep it quiet, strange, and sincere.

This document exists to help choose the harder path.
